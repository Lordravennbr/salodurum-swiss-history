# Visual Asset Policy

## Purpose

Visual material is a core component of Salodurum Swiss History, but illustration must never be confused with historical evidence.

## Visual classifications

Every visual asset included in the library must belong to one of these classes:

### A — Evidence

Photographs of archaeological sites or objects, excavation drawings, inscriptions, manuscripts, historical maps, and other documentary evidence.

Required metadata:
- creator or institution;
- object/site/document identification;
- date or archaeological context when known;
- source;
- license or permission status;
- modifications made by SVB, if any.

### B — Historical reconstruction

A visual reconstruction derived from archaeological, geographical, architectural, textual, or material evidence.

It must state that it is a reconstruction and document the evidence used. Uncertain elements must not be disguised as known facts.

### C — Didactic illustration

A diagram or illustration created to explain routes, chronology, relationships, military organization, migration hypotheses, linguistic change, or other concepts.

It must not be presented as a literal depiction of an undocumented event.

## Historical maps

Maps should use separate layers for:

- modern geography;
- modern borders used only for orientation;
- confirmed ancient sites;
- documented Roman roads or routes;
- approximate historical zones;
- disputed or hypothetical areas;
- chronology/date represented by the map.

Ancient ethnic or political territories must not be drawn with modern-style precise borders unless the evidence genuinely supports such precision.

## Comparative maps

Where useful, the project should pair a historical reconstruction map with a modern reference map covering the same geographic extent. This allows readers to understand where ancient sites and cultural zones correspond to present-day Switzerland and neighboring countries.

## AI-assisted illustrations

AI-assisted artwork may be used only as clearly labelled didactic or reconstruction imagery. It cannot be used as evidence and must not be captioned in a way that implies it is an authentic ancient, archaeological, or archival image.

## Third-party rights

Third-party visual material is included only when its reuse rights are known and compatible with repository distribution, or when explicit permission has been obtained. Attribution and license metadata must travel with the asset.
