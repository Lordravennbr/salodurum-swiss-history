# Editorial Policy

## Sonic Vantage Bibliotec — Salodurum Swiss History

### Core rule: No source → no claim

Salodurum Swiss History is intended to become a serious multilingual historical research library. Historical statements must not be presented as established fact unless they are supported by identifiable, verifiable evidence.

## Evidence classes

Every substantial historical claim should be treated according to one of the following categories:

1. **Verified** — supported by reliable archaeological, academic, institutional, or primary-source evidence.
2. **Probable** — supported by evidence but containing a meaningful degree of scholarly uncertainty.
3. **Disputed** — credible sources or scholarly interpretations disagree.
4. **Hypothesis** — a reasoned reconstruction that is not established fact.
5. **Insufficient evidence** — available evidence does not justify a conclusion.

If evidence is insufficient, the project must stop at that boundary rather than inventing a continuation.

## Source hierarchy

Preference should normally be given to:

1. archaeological reports and official archaeological services;
2. peer-reviewed academic research and scholarly monographs;
3. critical editions and translations of primary sources;
4. museums, archives, universities, historical dictionaries, and recognized research institutions;
5. reputable secondary reference works.

News reports and general websites may help locate a discovery or publication but should not replace the underlying archaeological or scholarly source when that source is available.

## Primary sources

Ancient and medieval written sources must be identified by author, work, passage where practical, approximate date, and translation/edition used. Statements by ancient authors are evidence for what a source reports; they are not automatically treated as objective descriptions of events or peoples.

## Archaeology

Archaeological interpretation must distinguish the excavated evidence from the interpretation placed upon it. A grave good, building foundation, coin, weapon, inscription, ceramic assemblage, or burial practice must not automatically be used to assign modern ethnicity, nationality, language, or identity.

## Peoples and identity

Terms such as Helvetii, Rauraci, Burgundians, Alemanni, Franks, Romans, Gallo-Romans, and Romani must be used in their historical context. They must not be treated as simple equivalents of modern Swiss, French, German, or other national identities.

## Maps

Historical maps produced by the project must distinguish documented locations from approximate zones and hypothetical reconstructions. Modern political borders may be shown only as reference overlays and must not be presented as ancient borders.

## Illustrations

Reconstructed scenes must be labelled as reconstructions. AI-assisted or digitally created illustrations must never be presented as archaeological photographs or direct evidence.

## Multilingual editions

The project is maintained in English, German, French, Italian, and Brazilian Portuguese. Translation must preserve evidentiary qualifiers such as “probably,” “possibly,” “according to,” “disputed,” and “unknown.” Translation must never strengthen an uncertain claim into a fact.

## Corrections

Historical accuracy takes priority over preserving previous wording. When stronger evidence becomes available, the library should be corrected and the Git history will preserve the earlier version.
